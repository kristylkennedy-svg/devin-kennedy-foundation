The case against drowning

Why the official story does not hold

The argument

Undetermined is not drowning.
Closed is not solved.

Jackson County closed this case on the strength of one word: drowning. Two pathologists refused to write that word. The grand jury's own report admits it was reached by elimination, from a body too decomposed to test, with a head injury nobody can explain. Everything else rests on the accounts of the people who left him on the island, and their own messages show them asking where he was, deleting conversations, and discussing lawyers.

This page does not accuse anyone of a crime. No one has been charged, and the grand jury found no credible evidence of one. What it does is set the official story next to the record, line by line, and let you decide whether "drowning, case closed" is an answer or an exit.

The official story

To accept it, you have to believe all of this

  1. That a strong swimmer and college athlete, who had never once left his group, chose to stay alone on an island with no water, no shelter and no phone service.
  2. That he did it without his phone, which stayed on the boat that left him.
  3. That the friends who said "everyone is aboard" on a distress call counted correctly, and that the boat was both sinking and fine within minutes.
  4. That nobody noticed him missing for more than six hours after the boat docked, and nobody asked where he was in the group chat until after midnight.
  5. That the bruising on the back of his head, found by two separate pathologists, came from a slip nobody saw hit anything.
  6. That the Snapchat messages deleted from his group chats while he was missing were routine, and that it does not matter who deleted them or what they said.
  7. That a toxicology report the grand jury itself called "not particularly informative" is enough to say how he died.
  8. That the sheriff was right to announce no foul play on the day the body was found, 24 days before the state autopsy was finished.

Sources: ABC News, grand jury report, ABC News, July 27, Mississippi Free Press, PBS NewsHour, Oxygen, CNN timeline, LAmag

Side by side

What a solved drowning looks like. What this one looks like.

Drowning

If this were a solved drowning

A finding, backed by evidence

What the record actually says

A diagnosis of exclusion. The grand jury's own report says it was reached "by process of elimination" and that decomposition made even that harder.

Sources: ABC News, grand jury report

The cause of death

If this were a solved drowning

Determined

What the record actually says

Undetermined. Twice. The state medical examiner and an independent pathologist with no ties to Mississippi both declined to call it drowning.

Sources: ABC News, Oct. 5, Mississippi Free Press

The head injury

If this were a solved drowning

Explained

What the record actually says

Unexplained. Two dark red-purple bruises under the scalp on the back of his head, in both autopsies. The report says how he got them "remains unclear."

Sources: ABC News, Oct. 5, ABC News, grand jury report

Intoxication

If this were a solved drowning

Measured

What the record actually says

Unmeasurable. Toxicology was "not particularly informative" because decomposition fluid cannot measure substance levels. The only evidence he was drunk is what the people who left him said.

Sources: ABC News, grand jury report, ABC News, July 27

His last hours

If this were a solved drowning

Accounted for

What the record actually says

Blank after 2:23 p.m. The last unlock of his phone is 2:18. The last video is 2:23. Everything after that is other people's word.

Sources: Mississippi Free Press, CNN timeline

The decision to stay

If this were a solved drowning

Confirmed

What the record actually says

Contradicted. Friends say he stayed for a young woman. The family says she told them he went back to the boat. In the chat, the friends themselves called the people around her "all so suspicious."

Sources: LAmag, Oxygen, ABC News, July 27

The evidence

If this were a solved drowning

Reviewed in full

What the record actually says

Disputed. The examiner says the DA got all 282 messages and the full data. The DA says she got a 300-page summary, not the 30-hour extraction. Snapchat's own records have never been subpoenaed, as far as the public record shows.

Sources: Mississippi Free Press

The conduct

What the people who left him did next

We do not name them. They have not been charged, and people have been federally charged for threatening them. Their actions are a matter of record, and the record is what an investigation is for.

  1. 4:00 p.m.

    "Everyone is aboard."

    On the distress call to Sea Tow, the caller says about seven people are on the boat and all are accounted for. Nolan is not on the boat. It leaves at 4:31 without him.

    Sources: PBS NewsHour, ABC News, July 27

  2. 4:31 to 6:00 p.m.

    Sinking, then fine, then full speed.

    The boat crawls nearly three miles at under 4.2 knots while a relative tells Sea Tow the problem is fixed. At 5:25 it runs at 30 to 35 knots and is home in 20 minutes. Three different accounts of who towed it.

    Sources: ABC News, July 27, PBS NewsHour

  3. 6:00 to 11:00 p.m.

    Five hours of silence.

    The boat docks around 6. It is out of the water at the owner's house by 7:19. Nolan's parents get a call around 11. A call to the Coast Guard that night does not request its help.

    Sources: CNN timeline

  4. After midnight

    "Has anybody seen Nolan yet."

    The group chat only starts asking where he is after midnight. At 6:15 a.m. someone asks who gave him a ride back. Before noon: "There is definitely some sus s--- going on."

    Sources: Oxygen, CBS News

  5. 2:18 p.m. to 3:14 a.m.

    Messages vanish.

    Between Nolan's last unlock and the moment his parents retrieve his phone, Snapchat messages in his group chats are deleted remotely by other users. Who, and what they said, is known only to Snapchat.

    Sources: Mississippi Free Press

  6. July 6

    "Ask for an attorney."

    The day Nolan is found, the chat turns to how to handle police: "the smart move is to ask for an attorney," "Only facts," "just stick to what you know happened." Later: "We have nothing to hide."

    Sources: Oxygen

  7. July 6

    The first public account comes from a judge.

    The mother of one friend, a Jackson County chancery judge, posts the timeline that becomes the official story: last seen around 3, chose to stay, planned a ride with another group. The case is investigated and closed inside Jackson County.

    Sources: TheGrio

The officials

The people who closed this case

These are public officials. They made these decisions in public, on the public's behalf, and they answer to the public. Their names, their decisions, and what they still owe Nolan's family.

Jackson County Sheriff's Office

Sheriff John Ledbetter

What they did. Declared there were no signs of foul play on July 6, the day Nolan was found and before any autopsy. Said the investigation would be "thorough and factual." Never named a suspect, a person of interest, or an investigator.

What they owe his family. Explain what the July 6 finding of no foul play was based on. Release the investigative file now that the case is closed.

Sources: CNN timeline, TheGrio, July 14

Jackson County

District Attorney Angel Myers McIlrath

What they did. Presented the case to a grand jury and declared it closed. Says her office received a 300-page summary from the family's examiner, not the full phone extraction. Says the family's team told her it had no evidence of a crime. Has not announced a subpoena for Snapchat's records.

What they owe his family. Subpoena Snapchat. Obtain and review the complete 30-hour extraction. Say publicly whether the other boats' GPS and the friends' phones were examined.

Sources: Mississippi Free Press, ABC News, grand jury report

State of Mississippi

Mississippi State Medical Examiner's Office

What they did. Ruled the cause and manner of death undetermined. Found bruising on the back of the head it could not explain. Removed the lungs and neck structures before the independent pathologist could examine them.

What they owe his family. Release the full autopsy report and explain why the organs were not preserved for a second examination.

Sources: ABC News, Oct. 5, CNN timeline

State of Mississippi

Attorney General Lynn Fitch

What they did. Has the authority to review a county prosecution. Has made no public statement on Nolan's case.

What they owe his family. Say whether the state will review the county's handling of the case.

Sources: WLOX

State of Mississippi

Governor Tate Reeves

What they did. Criticized the family's attorney on X and backed the county investigation while it was still open.

What they owe his family. Explain why the state backed the county's conclusions before the autopsy was complete.

Sources: CNN timeline

Where that leaves it

A boy went to an island with his friends and came back in a body bag, and the only official answer is a word two doctors would not write.

The grand jury's report says the case should be re-presented if credible evidence of a non-accidental death emerges. Mississippi has no statute of limitations on homicide. Horn Island is federal land. The evidence that could settle this exists. It has not been looked at by anyone outside Jackson County.

Sources: ABC News, grand jury report, ABC News, Oct. 5, WLOX
